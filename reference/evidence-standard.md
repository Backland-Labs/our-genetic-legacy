# Evidence standard

This is the rule set every agent applies. It follows the professional genealogy standard: reasonably exhaustive research, complete citations, correlation of evidence, resolution of conflicts, and a written conclusion. DNAversity does not lower the standard for beginners. It explains it more.

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed / Verified** | Meets the full threshold below and the participant has accepted it. |
| **Strongly Supported** | Multiple independent evidence streams agree. No material unresolved conflict. Threshold not yet complete (the participant has not reviewed it). For a consequential identity this status also requires Ida's challenge; until then the cap is Probable. |
| **Documentary Supported** | Documentary evidence supports the relationship. DNA is unavailable or not applicable. |
| **DNA Supported** | DNA supports the relationship or branch. Documentary proof is incomplete. |
| **Probable** | Best current explanation, but at least one meaningful evidence gap remains. |
| **Possible / Unverified** | Plausible hypothesis. Insufficient evidence for stronger language. |
| **Conflicting** | Important evidence streams disagree and the conflict is unresolved. |
| **Disproven / Rejected** | Evidence rules out or materially contradicts the claim. |
| **Unknown** | No defensible conclusion yet. A valid and intentional state. |

For relationship claims captured during the Foundation, use the intake classes: Supported/Known, Reported, Alleged, Unknown, Conflicting, Non-Biological. They describe the claim as received. Pauli converts them to the statuses above once evidence is evaluated. A relationship of a historical person that comes from public sources rather than from the participant (an encyclopedia naming an ancestor's mother) is also class Reported, with the claim source "public sources" and the `E-` ids; its status is capped at Possible until an original record or a firsthand statement is seen, however many authored pages agree.

## The Confirmed threshold

Confirmed is never the result of one AI inference, one online tree, a surname, an ethnicity estimate, one DNA match, a platform prediction, one family story, or one unsupported document. All of the following must hold:

1. Sufficient evidence appropriate to the question supports the relationship.
2. More than one independent evidence stream is used when reasonably available.
3. No unresolved material conflict remains.
4. DNA is genetically compatible when DNA is relevant.
5. Documentary evidence supports the relationship when such evidence should reasonably exist.
6. Pauli has completed an evidence review.
7. Ida has performed a challenge review for any consequential or uncertain biological identity.
8. The participant has reviewed and accepted the conclusion.

A "consequential" identity is any parent or grandparent identity, any conclusion about a living person, any unexpected-parentage finding, and anything the participant says matters to them.

## Classifying what you are looking at

**Source** (the container):
- *Original*: the first recording of the information (a register entry, a certificate, a letter, a photograph, a DNA test result).
- *Derivative*: a copy, abstract, index, transcription, or database entry made from an original.
- *Authored*: a narrative or compilation created by someone reasoning from other sources (a family history, an online tree, a published genealogy).

**Information** (the statement inside the source):
- *Primary*: given by someone with firsthand knowledge of the event, close in time.
- *Secondary*: given by someone who learned it from others or long afterward. The informant on a death certificate usually gives secondary information about the deceased's parents.
- *Undetermined*: the informant or their knowledge cannot be established.

**Evidence** (how the information bears on the claim):
- *Direct*: answers the question by itself.
- *Indirect*: answers the question only when combined with other evidence.
- *Negative*: the absence of an expected record where it should exist.

## Independence

Two items are independent only if they trace to different original sources. Common traps:

- Five online trees that agree are often one tree copied four times.
- An index and the record it indexes are one source.
- A family story and a tree built from that story are one source.
- The same cousin matched on Ancestry and on MyHeritage is one piece of DNA evidence.
- A published county history and the obituary that quoted it are one source.

When you cannot tell whether items are independent, say so and count them once.

## What gets an E- id

Any item that bears on a claim gets an `E-` id so it can be cited and classified: records, images, index entries, DNA findings, and also family statements, oral history, and clues copied from a match's tree. The id does not make it strong; the source class does. A family statement is an authored source with secondary or undetermined information and is recorded that way. What does not get an `E-` id is a bare lead with no content yet ("there is a tree with 400 people"). That is a task.

## Leads versus evidence

These are leads. They tell you where to look. They are never, by themselves, the basis for a status above Possible:

- Online trees, hints, and "potential parent" suggestions
- Index entries not checked against an image
- Ethnicity or admixture estimates
- A surname shared by matches
- A platform's predicted relationship
- A family story
- A lineage-society database entry
- An AI transcription that has not been checked against the image

## Writing the reason

Every status carries a reason a beginner can follow. Pattern: what the evidence is, why it is strong or weak, what would change the status. Example:

> Status: Probable. Two original records (the 1950 census and Loretta's birth certificate) name the same parents, and the informant on the birth certificate was the mother herself. But the 1950 census lists Loretta as "granddaughter," which is unexplained. A marriage record for the named parents, or a second household record naming Loretta as their daughter, would move this to Strongly Supported.
