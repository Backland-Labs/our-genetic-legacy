# Glossary

Define these the first time they appear for a beginner. Keep the definitions this short.

- **Anchor**: a known relative among your DNA matches whose relationship has been checked against the DNA. Anchors tell you which side of the family a group of matches belongs to.
- **Autosomal DNA**: the DNA tested by Ancestry, 23andMe, MyHeritage, FamilyTreeDNA's Family Finder, and Living DNA. Inherited from both parents.
- **Centimorgan (cM)**: the unit testing companies use to say how much DNA two people share. More usually means closer, with a lot of overlap between relationships.
- **Cluster / group**: matches who share DNA with each other, usually because they descend from the same ancestral couple.
- **Collateral relative**: a relative who is not a direct ancestor, such as an ancestor's sibling. DNA matches often descend from collateral lines.
- **Derivative source**: a copy, index, or transcription of an original record.
- **Direct evidence**: evidence that answers the question by itself.
- **Endogamy**: generations of marriage within a limited community, which makes relatives share more DNA than expected.
- **FAN club**: friends, associates, and neighbors of the person you are researching. When a person's own records are missing, the people around them often lead to the answer.
- **GEDCOM**: a standard file format for family-tree data that most genealogy programs can export and import.
- **Indirect evidence**: evidence that answers the question only when combined with other evidence.
- **Informant**: the person who supplied the information on a record. Who the informant was decides how reliable the information is.
- **Negative evidence**: the absence of a record where one should exist.
- **Original source**: the first recording of information, such as a register entry or a certificate.
- **Pedigree collapse**: when cousins married, so a person has fewer distinct ancestors than expected and some ancestors appear twice in the tree.
- **Primary information**: given by someone with firsthand knowledge, close to the time of the event.
- **Readiness state**: Harriet's summary of whether the foundation is solid enough to research a question.
- **Secondary information**: given by someone who learned it from others or long afterward.
- **Shared match**: a third person who matches both you and one of your matches. Different sites call this Shared Matches, Relatives in Common, In Common With, or Shared DNA Matches.
- **Triangulation**: showing that three or more people share the same segment of DNA, which supports a common ancestor. Overlap on a chromosome browser is not the same thing.
- **Unknown**: a valid entry meaning no defensible conclusion yet. It is never filled in with a guess.
