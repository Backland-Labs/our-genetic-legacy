# FOUND-01 — Family & DNA Foundation

**Owner:** Harriet. **Input:** conversation. **Output:** a Foundation summary in the case file and a readiness state.

## Why this comes first

The Foundation decides whether later research means anything. A research question about a great-grandfather depends on three parent-child links being real. If one of them is not, the question is about someone else's family. Establishing what is known, and how it is known, is what lets every later conclusion stand.

## Fast path

If the participant's first message already contains facts, or they answer several questions at once, do not walk them through the list below. Record what they gave, show it back as a short table with Unknown where it is unknown, ask for at most one load-bearing thing that is missing, and if they say they have nothing more, declare the Foundation complete and move to triage and readiness. The list below is for a conversational intake, not a checklist to complete.

## Questions, one generation at a time

Ask conversationally, not as a form. Stop wherever knowledge stops. Unknown is a complete answer and is recorded as such.

**The tester**
- Whose family history are we researching? Their own, or someone else's with permission?
- Alias to use in the case file.
- Approximate birth year and place (decade and state or country is enough for a beginner).

**Parents**
- Mother: name or alias, living or deceased, biological or social (adoptive, step, guardian). How do you know? (personal knowledge, family statement, family story, record, online tree, DNA, other)
- Father: the same.

**Grandparents**, for each of the four:
- Name or alias, living or deceased, approximate dates and places, biological or social, how you know.

**Great-grandparents**, only where known. Do not push.

**Relationship classes** for each link: Supported/Known, Reported, Alleged, Unknown, Conflicting, Non-Biological. Record the class and the source of the claim together.

**Siblings and half-siblings** of the tester, and known siblings of the parents. Collateral lines are where DNA matches come from.

**DNA**
- Which sites has the tester used? Which profiles do they manage?
- Which known relatives have tested, on which sites?
- Has anyone else's kit been shared with them? Do they have that person's permission to use it?

**Records and stories**
- Any documents already in hand (certificates, obituaries, Bibles, photographs with names).
- Family stories that bear on the question, recorded as stories.

## Screen validation

There is no screen. Validate by reflecting the tree back in a short table and asking the participant to correct it before you record it. This table is the one table that belongs in the reply rather than the case file.

## Extraction

Create `P-` entries for each person, with relationship class and claim source. Create `K-` entries for each DNA profile. Create `E-` entries for documents in hand (to be processed by Carter). Record stories as notes attached to the relevant person, marked "family story."

## Fallback

If the participant cannot answer a question, record Unknown and move on.

**When a name is offered for an unknown parent.** "My mother says his name was Ray" does not identify a person, so the `P-` row stays Unknown. Record the statement as an `E-` item (family statement, secondary information), note it on the row, and open an `H-` hypothesis if the participant wants to pursue it. Use class **Alleged** only when the participant names a specific, identifiable person as the parent and is unsure whether it is true; then the row carries that person's alias with class Alleged and the claim source, and the status is Unknown until evidence is evaluated. The difference matters: a first name is a clue, an identified person is a claim.

**If the participant moves on without confirming the reflected-back tree**, record what they said with a note "reflected, not yet confirmed," proceed, and confirm it the next time the tree is displayed.

Before recording anything about living relatives, remind the participant to use first names or aliases.

## Evidence class

Everything gathered here is user assertion until a record or DNA supports it.
